Forecasting in the Wild: Holidays, Exogenous Signals, ML and Prophet

Forecast daily restaurant visitors when outside events matter: add exogenous signals to SARIMA, turn forecasting into a regression problem, try a tree-based model and Prophet, and choose one model through a fair comparison.

Case: Xotica517 days · 1 Jan 2016 – 31 May 2017SARIMAX · LightGBM · Prophet
About the dataThe file xotica_visitors.csv has 517 rows and four columns: date, weekday, holiday (1 for a holiday, 0 otherwise) and total, the number of visitors that day. The data is simulated for teaching. It describes no real restaurant, and its holiday column is Xotica's own calendar of public holidays and popular occasions. Every model on this page is scored on the same split: the first 436 recorded days to train, the last 42 days (6 weeks) to test.
1 · Cold open

The holiday the kitchen ran out

You are a data scientist at Xotica, a food chain with restaurants in the United States. On a recent holiday the kitchen ran out of food by eight in the evening. The manager had planned for an ordinary day. She now asks you to forecast the number of visitors for the next 39 days, using about a year and a half of daily records, and she adds one condition: no more surprises on holidays.

Start with the simplest sensible forecast from Class 3, the seasonal naive: every day of the coming weeks repeats the same weekday of the last known week.

The forecast knows last week's visitors. What does it not know about a holiday?
It does not know that the day is a holiday. Yet that fact is printed on the calendar months in advance. Today is about getting such outside information into a forecast. What other outside factors could change restaurant visitors? We sort them in Section 4.

The plan

1 Understand the daily data and fit a SARIMA baseline.
2 Add the holiday signal with SARIMAX.
3 Forecast with regression and LightGBM on engineered features.
4 Learn the two strategies for multi-day forecasts.
5 Try Prophet.
6 Compare everything fairly and choose one model.

3 · The baseline

SARIMA and what it misses

The data is seasonal, so a SARIMA model is the natural baseline. The number 436 has no special meaning; it simply leaves a 6-week test, close to the 39 days the manager asked for. The ADF p-value of the raw training series is 0.288, so we cannot call it stationary. The autocorrelation of the daily changes is far higher at lags 7, 14 and 21 (0.77, 0.76, 0.75) than at lag 1 (0.17), so the season length is m = 7.

Choosing the orders

The seasonal order is hard to read from ACF and PACF plots alone, so we search over a few values, as in Class 6.

SettingGuideline
mthe lag with the highest autocorrelation, ignoring lag 0 and the first few lags
P and Qkeep P + Q at 2 or less; larger models risk overfitting
D and dusually not above 2 in total; check stationarity with the ADF test

We start from order=(1, 1, 1) with seasonal_order=(1, 0, 1, 7).

def fit_sarimax(y, exog=None, order=(1, 0, 0), **options):
    settings = dict(exog=exog, order=order, seasonal_order=(1, 0, 1, 7), **options)
    try:
        return SARIMAX(y, **settings).fit(disp=False, maxiter=200)
    except np.linalg.LinAlgError:       # backup start-up method, used only if the default fails
        return SARIMAX(y, initialization="approximate_diffuse", **settings).fit(disp=False, maxiter=200)
NoteA seasonal AR weight very close to 1 (here 0.990) can make the default start-up calculation fail with LinAlgError on some library versions. The wrapper then refits with another start-up method. If that happens, the scores of that model will differ from the ones written here.

4 · Outside information

Exogenous variables and SARIMAX

An exogenous variable is an input that helps to explain the target but is not itself influenced by it. A holiday changes the number of visitors; the number of visitors does not change the calendar.

BusinessExogenous variableIdea
Xoticaholidayholidays bring more visitors
Mobile phonesnew model launchedset to 1 for 2 or 3 months after a launch, to capture the spike
Sweatersmonth of the yeara seasonal exogenous variable: more sales in November to January
Street foodis it rainingsamosa sales are higher on rainy days

SARIMAX is SARIMA with such inputs; the X stands for exogenous. It keeps the seven hyperparameters (p, d, q)(P, D, Q)s and takes the extra columns in the exog argument. Each column gets a weight wi, learnt during fitting:

yt = w1x1,t + w2x2,t + ⋯ + ut,   ut follows a SARIMA model

SARIMA is a driver who only looks in the rear-view mirror. SARIMAX also reads the road signs, and a road sign is useful only because it tells you about the road ahead.

A common mistake

Suppose a model was fitted without the holiday column, and we pass the column only when predicting. Nothing changes: a model that was fitted without exog has no weight for it and silently ignores it (np.allclose is True). The variable must be given at fit time and again at forecast time.

sarimax = fit_sarimax(train["total"], exog=train[["holiday"]], order=(1, 1, 1))
fc_x = sarimax.forecast(42, exog=test[["holiday"]])       # two brackets: a table with one column
Visualizer · adding the holiday signal

The model learnt that a holiday adds about 45 visitors. With the same orders as before, one extra column moves the forecast up on the red lines, lowers the overall MAPE and cuts the holiday error by more than half (27.30% to 12.98%). The MA term near −1 suggested that d = 1 is not needed, so we fit the version without it as well: on these test days the simpler (1,0,0) version is slightly behind (1,1,1). One test window cannot settle such a small gap; Section 10 compares them on several windows.

The future values of exog must be known

To forecast, the model needs the exogenous value of every future day. Without them the library refuses to forecast (ValueError: Out-of-sample operations in a model with a regression component require additional exogenous values). Before adding any variable, ask: will I know it on the day I make the forecast?

VariableKnown 39 days ahead?What to do
Holiday calendaryesuse it directly

Weatheronly as another forecast, and weak far aheadits error adds to yours
Same-day online ordersnodo not use it for this forecast

5 · Regression

Regression for time series: lags, averages and calendar features

Can ordinary regression forecast a time series? Yes, if we turn each day into a row of features that are known before that day. SARIMA is a specialist with fixed rules about the past. Regression is a general worker: it learns from whatever columns you hand to it. Your job moves from choosing orders to designing columns.

  • Day type: the seven weekday names become two groups (weekend, working day), and get_dummies turns the group into two 0/1 columns.
  • Lags: Lagi is the number of visitors 𝑖 days earlier.
  • Rolling averages of the last week, two weeks and month. For an average alone, shift(1).rolling(31).mean() is shorter than 31 lag columns and gives the same numbers. The shift(1) matters: the average must end yesterday.
  • Weekday average: the average visitors on that day of the week.
Visualizer · the leakage trap

The two columns differ a little. The first one is leakage: it uses the visitors of the test days to build a feature for those same days. The differences look small, but the habit is wrong, and on other data the effect can be large. We use the training-only version.

ImportantEvery statistic used as a feature, such as a mean per weekday, must be computed from the training period only.
Should the date itself be a feature?
Not as a label. A raw date only identifies the row. What helps is something derived from it that repeats or is known in advance: day of the week, month, holiday flag, or a running day number for a trend.
def history_features(s):
    # everything here is known at the end of day t
    return pd.DataFrame({"latest": s, "avg_7": s.rolling(7).mean(),
                         "avg_14": s.rolling(14).mean(), "avg_31": s.rolling(31).mean()})

def calendar_features(index, weekday_mean):
    c = pd.DataFrame(index=index)
    c["holiday"] = full.loc[index, "holiday"].values
    c["dayofweek"] = index.dayofweek
    c["weekday_mean"] = weekday_mean.loc[index.dayofweek].values
    return c

def one_step_table(s, weekday_mean):
    table = history_features(s).shift(1)                 # history up to yesterday
    table = table.join(calendar_features(s.index, weekday_mean))
    table["total"] = s
    return table.dropna()

447 usable rows and 7 features: yesterday's visitors (latest), three averages, and three calendar features. total is the target.

Visualizer · linear regression, one day ahead

A plain linear regression reaches a MAPE of 11.27%, with a holiday weight of about 43 visitors. The weekday average has a weight close to 1, and the recent averages describe the level.

ImportantDo not compare this number with SARIMAX yet. SARIMAX forecast 1 to 42 days ahead from one starting point. This regression forecasts only one day ahead: for every test day it uses the real visitors of the day before. The next section repairs this.

Ways to improve a regression forecaster: design better features, select features with care, try other regressors and tune their hyperparameters, and combine models, for example by stacking or cascading.

6 · Many days ahead

Multi-day horizons: direct and recursive forecasting

The manager needs 39 days now, not one day at a time. On the day we forecast, we do not know yesterday's visitors for day 20. There are two standard strategies.

RecursiveTrain one one-step model. Predict day 1, treat that prediction as history, predict day 2, and so on.
ŷt+1 = f(yt, yt−1, …, xt+1)
ŷt+2 = f(ŷt+1, yt, …, xt+2)
DirectTrain one model per horizon h. Each uses only the history known at day 𝑡, plus the calendar of the target day.
ŷt+h = fh(yt, yt−1, …, xt+h),   h = 1, …, H
RecursiveDirect
Models to train1H, here 42
Weak pointerrors feed into later stepsfewer training rows and more variance at long horizons
Costlowgrows with the horizon

In table terms, the direct strategy shifts the target: for horizon h, the row for origin day 𝑡 gets the target total.shift(-h). Recursive is like passing a message along a line of people: one model, but a small mistake travels down the line. Direct is like phoning each person yourself: no passing on of mistakes, but many calls.

Visualizer · two strategies, one linear model
MAPE by week of the horizon

Both functions receive only the training series and the holiday calendar, so these scores are fair 42-day forecasts. The recursive version scores 11.04% and the direct version 11.11%, both close to the one-day-ahead 11.27%. Errors are lowest in week 1 and higher in the middle weeks, then low again in week 6. They do not grow steadily: here the calendar features carry much of the signal, and the calendar is just as reliable 40 days ahead as 1 day ahead. On a series driven mainly by its recent values, expect the error to grow with the horizon.

NoteA one-day-ahead score is not automatically better than a multi-day score on one window, as here. It is still a different task, so the two must never be ranked together.
7 · Trees

Tree-based forecasting with LightGBM

A linear regression adds up weighted features. Decision trees split the data with questions such as "is it a holiday?" or "is the 7-day average above 140?", so they can capture interactions that a straight line misses. LightGBM is a fast gradient boosting library: it builds many small trees, each one correcting the errors left by the earlier ones.

FM(x) = F0 + η Σm=1..M fm(x),   fm is a small tree and η the learning rate
SettingMeaning
n_estimatorsnumber of trees M
learning_ratehow much each tree is trusted, η
num_leavessize of each tree
min_child_samplessmallest number of rows in a leaf

For forecasting, nothing else changes: we reuse the same features and the same two strategies.

Visualizer · what the model used

One day ahead, LightGBM scores 10.25%, better than the 11.27% of linear regression on the same rows. The chart shows which features reduced the training error the most (share of total gain), and the day of the week is far ahead, which matches Section 2. Importance tells us what the model used, not what causes visitors.

Visualizer · four models over 42 days

Over 42 days the order flips: LightGBM recursive scores 11.49% and direct 13.83%, both behind the linear regression (11.04% and 11.11%). With about 400 training rows and mostly additive effects, the flexible model has little extra pattern to find and more room to fit noise. Tree models tend to earn their place with many rows, many related series and many useful features.

What trees cannot do

A tree predicts with averages of training targets collected in its leaves. See what happens on a series that keeps rising: 120 points of a rising line with noise, training on the first 90.

Visualizer · trees do not extrapolate

The future rises to 294, but every LightGBM prediction is 217, near the top of what it saw in training (230). Its error is 40.3 against 3.5 for the straight line. Tree models do not extrapolate a trend. Common remedies are to predict the change or the ratio to a recent average, or to remove the trend first and add it back.

8 · Prophet

Components, seasonalities, regressors and holidays

SARIMAX holds only one season length. Prophet, an open-source library from Facebook (now Meta), was designed for business series with several seasonal patterns and holidays. The method is described in the paper "Forecasting at Scale" (peerj.com/preprints/3190). It is a decomposable model:

y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + εt
PartMeaning
g(t)trend: piecewise linear or logistic growth, for non-periodic change
s(t)periodic changes, such as weekly and yearly seasonality
h(t)holiday effects on irregular dates, supplied by the user or built in
εterror: what the model does not explain

Each seasonality is a Fourier series, a sum of sine and cosine waves with period 𝑃, so several periods can be added together:

s(t) = Σn=1..N [ an cos(2πnt / P) + bn sin(2πnt / P) ]

The formula looks like SARIMAX with a trend, but it works differently. SARIMAX predicts from yesterday and last week. Prophet draws curves over time: a trend curve, a weekly curve, a holiday bump, and adds them. What makes it popular: easy parameters, tolerance to missing days and trend shifts, usually good handling of outliers, multiple seasonalities, extra regressors and little feature work.

Prophet needs a table with a date column named ds (in datetime format) and a target column named y. y has 478 values among the 517 rows; Prophet can ignore missing values of y during fitting. Fit it with no extra settings, and make_future_dataframe creates the rows for the days we want to predict.

m = Prophet()
m.fit(P_train[["ds", "y"]])
future = m.make_future_dataframe(periods=42, freq="D")
forecast = m.predict(future)     # yhat, yhat_lower, yhat_upper and one column per component

Without being asked, Prophet added a weekly seasonality. The light band is the uncertainty interval, 80% by default.

Visualizer · Prophet variants on the test days

ImportantThe default Prophet's MAPE on the training days themselves is 11.63%, much lower than its test error (16.43%). Prophet's predict returns fitted values for old dates too. Scoring those and calling it forecast accuracy is a serious mistake. Score only days that the model has not seen.

Regressors, extra seasons and built-in holidays

Give Prophet the holiday signal as a regressor, the same idea as exog: it learnt about 45 extra visitors on a holiday, close to the SARIMAX weight, and the holiday error fell to about 15%. Yet the overall MAPE went up to 17.64%, because the other days got worse. So the holiday signal is right and something else is wrong with the forecast. Section 9 finds it.

  • Monthly cycle added by hand (add_seasonality(name="monthly", period=30.5, fourier_order=5)) did not help (18.39% against 17.64%). Add a season only when there is a business reason and the data supports it.
  • Built-in US holidays scored 18.69%, worse than the default. The built-in list contains national public holidays (Christmas Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, and so on), and none of them falls inside the test window, while Xotica's own calendar has 6 occasions there: 17 Mar, 28 Mar, 1 Apr, 14 Apr, 16 Apr and 18 Apr.

Prophet can also look up holidays when you have no calendar, which is a good start. A calendar that matches your business is usually better.

9 · Trend flexibility

Prophet changepoints and trend flexibility

Prophet lets the trend bend at changepoints. It first places many candidate changepoints (25 by default, in the first 80% of the training data) where the slope is allowed to change, and keeps most of the changes very small. The parameter changepoint_prior_scale (default 0.05) controls how freely the trend may bend. A larger value makes it more flexible; too large overfits, too small underfits.

Visualizer · trend flexibility

The line for 0.05, the default, is the holiday-regressor model of Section 8. As the scale grows, the training error falls from 11.91% to 9.37%, while the test error rises from 10.34% to 25.13%. A flexible trend follows the last few weeks of the training data and then projects that direction into the future. A stiff trend (0.001) gives the best result on this window.

A setting that looks ambitious

Finally: yearly and weekly seasonality switched on, a 95% interval and a very flexible trend (scale 4), once with our regressor and once with built-in holidays.

Visualizer · yearly season on short data

These two settings have excellent training errors, near 9% (9.27% and 9.64%), and very poor test errors (36.15% and 47.70%), far worse than the seasonal naive (16.50%). With less than two years of data, a yearly pattern cannot be learnt: each part of the year has been seen about once. Prophet logs a warning about exactly this, which we silenced in Setup. Combined with a very flexible trend, the forecast runs away: the lowest forecasts are 19 visitors and −7 visitors.

Used with care, Prophet remains a strong tool: simple for beginners, good on strongly seasonal business series, tolerant of gaps and trend shifts, open to extra regressors, easy to interpret through its components, helpful for spotting changepoints, and a quick first model that needs almost no feature engineering.

ImportantWe have just tried many settings and read the test score each time. Picking the best of them is tuning on the test set. The next section checks the choice on other windows.

Recap

Key takeaways

  • Daily visitors have a weekly season and a strong holiday effect. SARIMA sees only the past, so it misses holidays.
  • An exogenous variable is an outside input. SARIMAX learns a weight for it, and its future values must be known when the forecast is made.
  • Regression can forecast once the past becomes features: lags, rolling averages, day-of-week features and a holiday flag. All of them must be free of leakage.
  • For many days ahead, use the recursive strategy (one model, predictions fed back) or the direct strategy (one model per horizon).
  • LightGBM captures interactions but cannot extrapolate a trend, and on small data it may not beat a linear model.
  • Prophet adds trend, seasonalities and holidays. It accepts regressors and built-in holidays, and changepoint_prior_scale controls how freely the trend bends.
  • Compare on the same split and horizon, then check several windows before choosing.

Common mistakes
